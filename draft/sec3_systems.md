## 3. Current Humanoid Systems

The global humanoid robot landscape in 2024–2026 comprises two largely distinct populations: commercial platforms designed for industrial labor and a set of academic research systems whose provenance spans two decades of bipedal locomotion research. This section surveys both populations, quantifies their capabilities in a unified comparison table, and characterizes the open-source software ecosystem on which future medical applications would depend. The central empirical finding of this section is that no confirmed clinical deployment of any full-size bipedal humanoid robot exists as of March 2026 \cite{atar2025humanoids,cho2026humanoid,cunha2025humanoid}.

### 3.1 Commercial Platforms

The commercial humanoid sector underwent a decisive architectural transition between 2022 and 2024. Earlier systems — including the original hydraulic Atlas platform and several first-generation prototypes — relied on hydraulic actuation for its high force density, at the cost of mechanical complexity, fluid-system maintenance, energy inefficiency, and noise. The generation of systems entering commercial deployment in 2024–2025 are uniformly electric, with brushless DC motors or high-torque servo actuators paired with custom gearboxes. This transition has reduced unit cost, simplified field maintenance, and enabled quieter operation — properties that are prerequisites for hospital environments — although no manufacturer has yet targeted healthcare as a primary deployment context \cite{cao2025humanoid}.

A second defining characteristic of the 2024–2026 commercial generation is LLM-native design. Twelve of the thirteen systems surveyed were designed from inception to support vision-language-action (VLA) models or large language model (LLM) interfaces for task specification. Language-conditioned operation — issuing instructions in natural language rather than explicit task programs — is a prerequisite for adaptive behavior in unstructured clinical environments. Unitree is additionally notable as the only major commercial manufacturer to release and maintain a fully open-source SDK (unitree\_sdk2, unitree\_ros, unitree\_rl\_gym), enabling third-party research without licensing agreements. All other commercial systems operate as closed proprietary platforms.

**Tier 1 — Production-scale deployment.** The five systems in this tier have each completed production-scale or sustained pilot deployments in industrial settings as of early 2026.

*Figure 02* (Figure AI, USA; released August 2024) stands at 168 cm and 70 kg, with 28 total degrees of freedom (DOF) and 16 DOF per hand pair — among the highest hand dexterity specifications in this tier. Payload capacity is 20–25 kg; battery runtime is approximately 5 hours. Figure AI initially co-developed the AI stack with OpenAI (speech-to-speech and vision-language-based task planning); following termination of that partnership in mid-2025, the system now runs a fully in-house end-to-end neural model (Figure Neural Networks). The documented deployment is a BMW manufacturing facility (Spartanburg, SC) where 11 months of operation contributed to the assembly of over 30,000 vehicles. No healthcare deployment has occurred.

*Tesla Optimus Gen 2* (Tesla, Inc., USA; December 2023) measures 173 cm and 57 kg, with 28+ body DOF and 11 hand DOF (Gen 2) — expanded to 22 DOF via Gen 3 hands entering mass production in January 2026. Maximum walking speed is 8 km/h. The platform runs Tesla's Full Self-Driving-derived neural network stack. Current deployments are internal to Tesla's Fremont and Austin factories, performing battery cell sorting, parts handling, and quality inspection. No clinical deployment has occurred. Publicly available documentation does not confirm any clinical deployment or regulatory submission for medical applications.

*Boston Dynamics Atlas (electric)* (Boston Dynamics / Hyundai Motor Group, USA; unveiled April 2024) weighs 89 kg with 28 DOF and a sustained payload of 30 kg (50 kg instantaneous), and is the most physically capable system in this comparison. The platform represents a planned transition from the hydraulic Atlas (retired April 2024), which served as the primary research reference for dynamic whole-body control for over a decade. A strategic partnership with Google DeepMind, announced at CES January 2026, is integrating Gemini Robotics 1.5 (VLA) and Gemini Robotics-ER 1.5 (planning) foundation models into the system. Target deployment is Hyundai Motor Group automotive manufacturing. No medical or hospital deployment has occurred.

*Agility Digit v4* (Agility Robotics, majority-owned by Amazon, USA; commercial deployment 2024) stands 175 cm and 65 kg, with approximately 3 DOF per arm and a simple gripper as the standard end-effector; payload is 16 kg per cycle. The platform includes 4 Intel RealSense depth cameras and confirmed LiDAR. Digit is the most commercially validated logistics humanoid: confirmed deployments include GXO Logistics (Flowery Branch, GA; over 100,000 totes moved) and Amazon fulfillment center pilots. Healthcare has been cited as a long-term expansion target but no clinical deployment has occurred.

*Unitree G1/H1* (Unitree Robotics, China) are the most important commercial systems for this paper's scope. The H1 (2023) stands 180 cm and 47 kg with 19 DOF and joints rated up to 360 Nm; its approximate price is $90,000. The G1 (May 2024) is 132 cm and 35 kg, with 23 DOF in the base configuration and up to 43 DOF in the EDU variant, and is priced at approximately $13,500–16,000. Both systems benefit from an active, openly licensed software ecosystem (discussed in Section 3.4). The G1 is the **only commercial humanoid with documented medical procedure research**: Atar et al. \cite{atar2025humanoids} at the UC San Diego Yip Lab teleoperated a Unitree G1 equipped with Inspire Gen4 dexterous hands to execute seven medical procedures — including auscultation, Leopold maneuvers, and ultrasound-guided injection — achieving a 70% task success rate with non-clinician operators. A separate study by Cho et al. \cite{cho2026humanoid} at Johns Hopkins University employed a Unitree G1 as an assistant in endoscopic surgery procedures. Both constitute academic research prototypes, not clinical deployments. No Unitree robot has been cleared or deployed as a clinical tool in any healthcare system.

**Tier 2 — Early commercial.** *1X NEO Beta* (1X Technologies, Norway; August 2024) is the lightest platform in this survey at 30 kg, with 75 total DOF and 22 DOF per hand, a battery runtime of approximately 4 hours, and a noise level specified at 22 dB — a potentially relevant characteristic for patient care environments. It is positioned as a home assistant product at $20,000 or $499/month by subscription. No clinical deployment has occurred. *Apptronik Apollo* (Apptronik, USA; August 2023) stands 173 cm and 72.6 kg, with a 25 kg arm payload and 4-hour hot-swappable battery; current pilots are in automotive assembly (Mercedes-Benz, March 2025) and logistics. *Sanctuary Phoenix Gen 8* (Sanctuary AI, Canada; January 2025) is 170 cm and 70 kg with 21 DOF per hand and 5 mN tactile force sensitivity — the most capable haptic sensing in the commercial tier — driven by the proprietary Carbon AI control system developed in collaboration with Microsoft. Current deployments are limited to the automotive sector (Magna). No clinical deployment of any Tier 2 system has occurred.

**Tier 3 — Development and pre-commercial.** *Fourier GR-2* (Fourier Intelligence, China; late 2024) stands 175 cm and 63–65 kg with 53 total DOF, 12 hand DOF per pair, and six tactile sensor arrays with FSA 2.0 actuators rated above 380 Nm. Fourier Intelligence has a healthcare-affiliated subsidiary (Fourier Rehab) with confirmed research collaborations with Shirley Ryan AbilityLab and Tan Tock Seng Hospital; however, these partnerships involve exoskeleton and rehabilitation devices, not the GR-2 humanoid platform. The GR-2 itself has no confirmed clinical deployment. *UBTECH Walker X* (UBTECH Robotics, China; mature platform as of 2024) is 130–145 cm and 63–77 kg with 41 DOF, dual RGB-D LiDAR, and multimodal LLM integration; UBTECH maintains a separate healthcare product line (wheeled mobility and companion devices) distinct from Walker X. *AgiBot A2* (AgiBot/Zhiyuan, China; 2024) is 175 cm and 55 kg with 49+ DOF and a visual fingertip sensing system; AgiBot achieved the highest estimated global humanoid unit shipments in 2025 (approximately 5,168 units per Omdia), all in industrial and service contexts. *NEURA 4NE-1* (NEURA Robotics, Germany; 2023–2024) is 170–180 cm and 60–80 kg with confirmed force-torque sensing at 0.1 N sensitivity; it is marketed for industrial and home use. No Tier 3 system has a confirmed clinical deployment.

**Deployment reality.** All thirteen commercial systems surveyed are deployed exclusively in industrial or logistics contexts as of March 2026. The sole departure from this pattern is the Unitree G1's use as a research prototype in two academic medical studies \cite{atar2025humanoids,cho2026humanoid}. These studies are pre-clinical research demonstrations, not hospital deployments or regulatory submissions. The gap between demonstrated capability in controlled industrial tasks and validated safety in patient-contact clinical environments remains the central barrier to medical deployment, and it is not a gap that any commercial manufacturer has yet formally addressed.

### 3.2 Academic and Research Platforms

Academic humanoid development predates the commercial wave by approximately two decades. The platforms discussed here are relevant to this review for three reasons: they constitute the prior art from which commercial systems derive, they are the context in which nearly all existing clinical and HRI evidence was produced, and one purpose-built system — RHP Friends — represents the closest existing demonstration of a humanoid performing nursing care tasks.

**RHP Friends** (CNRS-AIST Joint Robotics Laboratory / Kawasaki Heavy Industries, Japan/France) is the only academic humanoid explicitly designed and demonstrated for nursing care \cite{benallegue2025rhp}. At 168 cm and 54 kg with 30 DOF (excluding hands), it is human-scale and directly deployable in standard clinical environments without infrastructure modification. The defining architectural feature is a seamless switching mechanism between autonomous and teleoperated control modes: routine tasks such as navigation and object retrieval execute autonomously using locomanipulation and object detection pipelines, while non-routine or high-stakes tasks are handed off to a remote human operator via teleoperation. This hybrid architecture is a principled response to the reality that full autonomy for patient-contact tasks is not currently safe or validated. The system was demonstrated at IREX 2023 (International Robot Exhibition, Tokyo) over three days with nine sessions per day, executing two healthcare-relevant tasks: patient transfer assistance (autonomous mode) and circuit breaker operation representing adaptability to human-built environments (teleoperated mode). No patients were involved. This demonstration constitutes the most directly healthcare-relevant academic humanoid performance documented in the literature as of early 2026. RHP Friends inherits hardware and software lineage from the CNRS-AIST HRP series (HRP-2, HRP-4, HRP-5P), representing over 20 years of continuous development in bipedal humanoid research.

**NAO** (Aldebaran Robotics / SoftBank Robotics, France/Japan; V6 current) is the most extensively studied humanoid in healthcare research: over 1,000 peer-reviewed publications cite it as the experimental platform. At 58 cm and 5.6 kg with 25 DOF, it has been deployed in ASD therapy trials (reviewed in a 2024 Science Robotics meta-analysis), elderly care facilities for cognitive stimulation and companionship, psychiatric HRI studies (Frontiers 2025), and hospital waiting areas for patient information delivery. Over 19,000 units are distributed globally across 70 countries. *Scope caveat*: NAO's 58 cm height and 5.6 kg weight make it categorically unsuitable for physical adult care tasks — patient transfer, ambulation assistance, or bedside manipulation. All clinical evidence for NAO pertains to social and cognitive interaction, not physical assistance. NAO is not a full-size bipedal humanoid under the definition adopted by this paper.

**Pepper** (SoftBank Robotics / Aldebaran, France/Japan) is the most extensively deployed humanoid in operational healthcare settings: over 2,000 Japanese care facilities use or have used Pepper for dementia cognitive stimulation, companion interaction, reminiscence therapy, and hospital administrative tasks. Production was paused in June 2021 due to weak commercial demand; existing units remain active. *Scope caveat*: Pepper is 120 cm tall and moves on a three-wheeled omnidirectional base — it is not bipedal and does not meet the paper's scope definition of a humanoid. Its significance for this survey is as a baseline: the documented deployment scale of Pepper in care facilities represents the threshold of real-world acceptance and operational robustness that fully bipedal humanoids must ultimately justify exceeding. The fact that Pepper achieved this scale with a non-bipedal, non-ambulatory form factor is itself a finding about the relative value of bipedal locomotion for current care applications.

**iCub / iCub3** (Italian Institute of Technology, Italy; 2004–present) is 104 cm and approximately 23 kg with 54 DOF and full-body capacitive tactile skin. It is the primary fully open-source humanoid (hardware under GPL; software YARP middleware; GitHub: robotology/icub-main). iCub3 (2022) adds an immersive telepresence capability allowing an operator to embody the robot via VR, which has been cited as potentially applicable to remote patient consultation and rehabilitation teleoperation. Over 40 units are distributed to research laboratories worldwide. No confirmed clinical deployment exists; research use is concentrated in cognitive developmental robotics, sensorimotor learning, and HRI.

**Platforms without confirmed healthcare use.** TALOS (PAL Robotics, 175 cm, 32 DOF, 6 kg arm payload) is used for manipulation and torque-control research at European labs but has no documented healthcare applications. Valkyrie (NASA JSC, 187 cm, 44 DOF, SEA actuation) returned to JSC in 2024 after a decade at the University of Edinburgh; its stated future focus is planetary exploration. WALK-MAN (IIT, 185 cm) was designed for disaster response and its primary project concluded around 2018. TORO (DLR, 174 cm, 27–39 DOF) shares actuation lineage with the KUKA LBR iiwa arms used in surgical robotics, but TORO itself has no clinical applications. LOLA (TU Munich, 176 cm, 26 DOF) is a locomotion research platform with no healthcare applications. ASIMO (Honda) was discontinued in March 2022; its principal healthcare legacy is the walking assist devices it inspired, not direct clinical use.

### 3.4 Open-Source Software Ecosystem

A survey of prominent open-source repositories relevant to humanoid robotics yields a clear finding: no dedicated software stack exists for humanoid medical deployment. What does exist is a maturing ecosystem of general-purpose infrastructure that could in principle support future medical applications, but which has not been validated, tested, or designed for clinical use.

**Foundation models and VLA frameworks.** The highest-activity segment of the open-source humanoid software ecosystem is vision-language-action (VLA) and robot learning frameworks. LeRobot (huggingface/lerobot; 22,179 stars as of 2026-03-10; Apache 2.0) is the most widely adopted end-to-end robot learning framework, providing standardized dataset formats, supported algorithms including ACT, Diffusion Policy, and π₀, and direct hardware support for the Unitree G1. Physical Intelligence's openpi (10,551 stars; Apache 2.0) provides the π₀ flow-matching VLA foundation model pre-trained on over 10,000 hours of real robot demonstrations; the model achieves state-of-the-art results on ALOHA bimanual manipulation benchmarks and supports fine-tuning with minimal demonstrations. OpenVLA (openvla/openvla; 5,479 stars; MIT), a collaboration of Stanford, Berkeley, and Toyota Research Institute, is a 7B-parameter VLA model trained on 970,000 real-world demonstrations that outperforms RT-2-X on manipulation benchmarks with 7× fewer parameters. All three are general-purpose manipulation frameworks with no healthcare-specific training data, safety validation, or clinical task benchmarks.

**Simulation environments.** Genesis (Genesis-Embodied-AI/Genesis; 28,254 stars; Apache 2.0) is a unified differentiable physics simulation platform supporting rigid bodies, deformable objects, liquids, and granular materials — properties relevant to surgical simulation and pharmaceutical task modeling — released publicly in December 2024. Isaac Lab (isaac-sim/IsaacLab; 6,544 stars; BSD-3-Clause) is NVIDIA's GPU-accelerated RL and imitation learning framework, with direct support for Unitree G1 and H1 humanoids and validated sim-to-real transfer for locomotion tasks. Neither platform provides medical environment models, clinical workflow scenarios, or validated patient-contact physics.

**Unitree hardware ecosystem.** The Unitree GitHub organization (unitreerobotics) maintains nine publicly accessible repositories for G1 and H1 support: unitree\_rl\_gym (3,022 stars) for locomotion training; unitree\_sdk2 (932 stars) as the core communication layer; unitree\_ros (1,271 stars) for ROS integration; and unitree\_lerobot (568 stars) for imitation learning via the LeRobot framework. The most medically relevant repository is xr\_teleoperate (1,309 stars), which implements full-body teleoperation of G1/H1 via XR headsets (Apple Vision Pro, Meta Quest 3, PICO 4). This is the software component used in the Atar et al. teleoperation study \cite{atar2025humanoids}: remote expert control via XR is a plausible near-term deployment model for scenarios in which a human clinician directs a humanoid body at the bedside, combining human judgment with robotic reach and precision.

**Healthcare-specific software.** The only directly healthcare-targeted open-source repository in the humanoid/embodied AI space is ros4healthcare (SCAI-Lab/ros4healthcare; 8 stars; Apache 2.0), a ROS 4 package for physiological sensing integration (heart rate, respiration, EEG, EMG) into robot control loops, developed at ETH Zurich and the University of Alberta. Its low star count reflects a niche domain rather than low utility: physiological sensing is a prerequisite for safe patient-contact operation, as a humanoid must be able to monitor patient state during assisted transfer or rehabilitation tasks. No other ROS package addressing humanoid integration with clinical workflows has been identified.

**The infrastructure gap.** The software infrastructure for general-purpose humanoid manipulation is maturing rapidly: VLA models achieve high success rates on tabletop and bimanual benchmarks, simulation environments support realistic physics, and hardware SDKs for the leading research platform (Unitree G1) are openly available. However, four categories of infrastructure required for clinical deployment are entirely absent from the open-source ecosystem: a validated safety layer enforcing force and contact limits for patient-contact scenarios; a ROS integration package for humanoid interaction with clinical systems (EHR, nurse call, hospital communication protocols); an open dataset of humanoid demonstrations in clinical or simulated clinical environments; and an open simulator with validated medical environment models (hospital room geometry, patient anatomy proxies, clinical equipment). The transition from "general-purpose manipulation capability" to "clinically safe, task-verified medical assistance" is primarily a software and validation challenge, and it is one for which the open-source community has not yet produced foundational components.
